A friend texts you a photo from their iPhone, you tap it — and instead of a picture you get a grey box, an error, or a file called `IMG_1234.HEIC` that simply won't open. If you're on an Android phone, this is a common headache, and it's not your phone's fault. The photo is saved in **HEIC**, Apple's default format, and Android's support for it is patchy.

Here's what's going on and the quickest way to actually see the photo.

## Why HEIC is a problem on Android

Since 2017, iPhones and iPads have saved photos as **HEIC** (High Efficiency Image Container) by default. It's an efficient format — files are roughly half the size of a JPG at similar quality — but that efficiency came at the cost of universal compatibility.

Android's support for HEIC has improved over the years, but it's inconsistent:

- **It depends on your Android version and phone maker.** Newer Android versions and phones from Samsung, Google, and others can often *display* HEIC in the Photos or Gallery app. Older devices frequently can't.
- **It depends on the app.** Even if your Gallery opens a HEIC file, a messaging app, email client, or web upload form on the same phone might refuse it.
- **Editing and sharing can still fail.** A photo might preview fine but then won't attach, upload, or open in a third-party editor.

So "can my Android open HEIC?" doesn't have one answer — it varies by device and by app. That unpredictability is exactly why converting to a universal format is the reliable fix.

## How to get the HEIC file onto your phone first

Sometimes the trickier part is getting the actual `.heic` file, because messaging apps handle photos in different ways:

- **Received by text or a chat app:** Save the image to your phone (long-press → *Save* or *Download*). If it saved as a `.heic` file that won't open, run it through the converter above.
- **From email:** Download the attachment, then convert it.
- **From a shared album or cloud link:** Download the original file rather than a screenshot — a screenshot loses quality and resolution.

Once the file is on your phone, the browser-based converter handles the rest.

### A note on screenshots

If you just need to *see* a photo and don't need the original file, taking a screenshot of the preview is a quick hack — but you'll get a lower-resolution image and lose the full detail. For anything you want to keep, print, or re-share, convert the real HEIC file to JPG instead.

## Better yet: ask the sender to change one setting

If a particular friend or colleague sends you iPhone photos often, the smoothest long-term fix is on *their* end. iPhones can capture photos as JPG from the start:

1. On the iPhone, open **Settings → Camera → Formats**.
2. Tap **Most Compatible**.

From then on, their new photos are saved as JPG and will open on your Android without any conversion. (This only affects *future* photos — anything already in their library stays HEIC.)

There's also a share-time trick: when an iPhone user shares a photo, iOS often converts it to JPG automatically depending on how they send it. But that behavior isn't guaranteed across every app, which is why files still slip through as HEIC — and why having a converter handy is useful.

## What if I just want to view lots of HEIC files?

If you regularly deal with HEIC — say you switched from iPhone to Android and have a library full of them — you have a couple of options:

- **Convert them in batches** with the [HEIC to JPG converter](/heic-to-jpg) so your entire collection lives in a format every app understands.
- **Install a dedicated HEIC viewer** from the Play Store if you'd rather keep the files as HEIC and just view them. This works, but you'll still hit walls whenever you try to upload or share to an app that doesn't support the format — which is why many people convert once and stop worrying about it.

Also worth knowing: this whole problem isn't unique to Android. HEIC photos can [refuse to upload to websites](/blog/heic-wont-upload-to-websites) and [need extra steps to open on Windows](/iphone-heic-windows) too. Converting to JPG solves all of these at once.
