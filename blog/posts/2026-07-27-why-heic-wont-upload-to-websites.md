You're filling out a job application, a passport renewal, or a marketplace listing. You tap "Upload photo," pick the perfect picture from your iPhone — and the site coughs it back at you: *"File type not supported."* Or it uploads, but the image shows up broken. If this has happened to you, you've run into one of the most common headaches with modern iPhone photos: the **HEIC** format.

Here's why some websites won't take your HEIC files, and the fastest, most private way to fix it.

## The short version

Since 2017, iPhones and iPads have saved photos as **HEIC** (High Efficiency Image Container) by default. It's a great format — roughly half the file size of a JPG at the same quality — but it's newer than the web itself, and many websites simply weren't built to accept it.

Most upload forms are configured to accept a specific list of file types, usually **JPG, PNG, and sometimes GIF or WebP**. If HEIC isn't on that list, the site rejects the file before it ever reaches its server. The picture isn't broken or corrupted — it's just wearing a file format the website doesn't recognize.

## Why does my iPhone upload fine on *some* sites but not others?

This trips a lot of people up, and there's a genuinely interesting reason behind it.

When you upload a photo **directly from the Photos app inside Safari on your iPhone**, iOS often quietly converts the HEIC to a JPG for you on the way out. So the upload just works, and you never even see the word "HEIC." Many big platforms also transcode images on their end after you upload.

The trouble usually starts when the file travels as a **raw `.heic`** — for example:

- You **AirDropped** the photo to a Mac or transferred it to a Windows PC, then tried to upload from there.
- You saved it to a cloud folder or USB drive and uploaded the original file.
- A stricter web form checks the file's actual type and refuses anything that isn't JPG or PNG.

In those cases the original HEIC survives intact, and a picky website turns it away. Same photo, different journey — different result.

## Which sites are most likely to reject HEIC?

There's no universal list, but the pattern is predictable. You're more likely to hit a wall with:

- **Government and official forms** — passport, visa, DMV, and benefits portals often demand JPG or PNG and nothing else.
- **Job application and HR systems** — many are older enterprise tools with narrow file-type rules.
- **Older or smaller websites** — forums, local business sites, and legacy content systems.
- **Print and photo-lab order forms** — some still expect classic JPG.

Bigger, modern platforms (major social networks, most webmail) tend to handle HEIC gracefully — but you can't count on it, and the error message rarely explains what went wrong.

## Want to avoid this in the future?

If you're tired of converting one photo at a time, you can tell your iPhone to shoot in the more compatible format from now on:

1. Open **Settings → Camera → Formats**.
2. Choose **Most Compatible**.

Your camera will start saving JPGs instead of HEIC. The trade-off is larger files and losing a few HEIC-only perks (like the extra editing headroom and Live Photo storage). Note that this only affects **new** photos — everything already in your library stays HEIC, so you'll still convert those the next time they need to go somewhere.

Many people prefer the best of both worlds: **leave the camera on High Efficiency** to save storage, and just convert a copy to JPG whenever a website asks for one.
