Your Mac opens HEIC photos just fine — Preview, Finder previews and Photos all show them without a second thought. The trouble starts when you need to send one somewhere else: a website that rejects the upload, a colleague on Windows, or an old app that only knows JPG. The photo is fine; it's just saved in [Apple's HEIC format](/blog/what-is-a-heic-file), and the other end doesn't speak it.

The good news: **your Mac can make JPG copies without any download.** macOS has three separate built-in tools that convert HEIC to JPG, plus a way to batch hundreds at once. Here's each one, and when to reach for it.

## The short answer

**Right-click the file in Finder, choose Quick Actions → Convert Image, and pick JPEG.** That's the fastest method, it needs no app, and it handles a whole selection of files at once. For HEIC files that aren't on your Mac — or when you want the same conversion to work identically on any computer — **HeicQuick** (heicquick.com) does it in the browser, on your own device, with nothing uploaded.

## Method 1: Finder Quick Actions (fastest, and works on batches)

Since macOS Monterey, Finder can convert images without opening any app at all.

1. In **Finder**, select one HEIC file — or **⌘-click several** to grab a batch.
2. **Right-click** (or Control-click) the selection.
3. Choose **Quick Actions → Convert Image**.
4. Set **Format** to **JPEG**, choose an image size (Actual Size keeps full resolution), and click **Convert to JPEG**.

The JPGs appear right next to the originals, and your HEIC files stay untouched. There's a **Preserve Metadata** checkbox if you want to keep or strip the date, location and camera info. This is the method most Mac users never discover, and it's the best one for everyday use.

## Method 2: Preview (best when you want to control quality)

Preview — the app that opens when you double-click a photo — can export to JPG with a quality slider, which is handy when you care about file size.

1. Open the HEIC file in **Preview**.
2. Go to **File → Export**.
3. In the **Format** menu, choose **JPEG**. (Hold **Option** while opening that menu to reveal extra formats.)
4. Drag the **Quality** slider, then click **Save**.

To convert several at once in Preview, select them all in Finder, open them together (they'll appear in one window's sidebar), select every thumbnail in that sidebar, then use **File → Export Selected Images**.

## Method 3: The Photos app

If your pictures live in the **Photos** library rather than as loose files, export them straight from there:

1. In **Photos**, select the photo or photos.
2. Choose **File → Export → Export [N] Photos**.
3. Set **Photo Kind** to **JPEG**, pick a quality, and click **Export**.

Photos writes out fresh JPG copies to wherever you choose, leaving the originals in your library as space-saving HEIC. Note that simply *dragging* a photo out of Photos may hand off the original HEIC — using **Export** is what guarantees a JPG.

## Method 4: An Automator Quick Action (set-and-forget batches)

Convert HEIC constantly? Build a one-click tool with **Automator** (it's pre-installed):

1. Open **Automator** and create a new **Quick Action**.
2. Set "Workflow receives" to **image files** in **Finder**.
3. Add the **Change Type of Images** action and set the type to **JPEG**. (Add a **Copy Finder Items** action first if you want to keep the originals.)
4. Save it as something like "Convert to JPG."

Now it lives in the right-click **Quick Actions** menu for any file you select — a batch converter you built in about a minute.

## Does converting lose quality?

Barely, and not in a way you'll notice. HEIC and JPG both use lossy compression, so a conversion re-encodes the image once — but at normal quality settings the difference is invisible on screen or in print. We go deeper in [does converting HEIC to JPG reduce quality](/blog/does-converting-heic-to-jpg-reduce-quality). If you're editing and want a copy with *no* extra compression at all, export to [PNG](/heic-to-png) instead of JPG.

## Stop the problem at the source

If the HEIC files come from *your own* iPhone or iPad, you can switch the camera to capture JPG from now on: on the phone, open **Settings → Camera → Formats** and choose **Most Compatible**. New photos save as JPG; existing ones stay HEIC, so you'll still convert those the first time. The trade-off is roughly double the storage per photo.
