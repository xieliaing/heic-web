You're trying to add a photo to a job application, a marketplace listing, an insurance claim, or a website form — and it just won't go through. Maybe the upload button greys out, maybe you get an error like *"file type not supported,"* or maybe nothing visibly happens at all. If the photo came from your iPhone or iPad, there's a good chance the culprit is the file format: **HEIC**.

Here's why it happens, and the fastest way to get your photo uploaded.

## The short version

Most websites were built to accept **JPG** and **PNG** — the two image formats that have worked everywhere for decades. HEIC is newer, and a lot of upload forms simply don't recognize it yet. When the site checks your file and sees `.heic`, it rejects it before the upload even starts.

Your photo isn't broken. It's just wearing a format the website doesn't know how to read. Convert it to JPG and the same picture uploads without a hitch.

## Why HEIC gets rejected

Since 2017, iPhones and iPads have saved photos as **HEIC** (High Efficiency Image Container) by default. It's a great format — the files are roughly half the size of a JPG at the same quality — but that efficiency comes at the cost of compatibility. A few things tend to go wrong at upload time:

### The site only allows specific file types

Many forms restrict uploads to a fixed list, often just `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, and `.png`. If HEIC isn't on the list, the file is blocked. This is the most common reason, and it's why the error usually mentions "supported file types."

### The server can't process the image

Even when a site *accepts* the upload, its behind-the-scenes tools may not be able to read HEIC — so it can't create the thumbnail or resized version it needs, and the upload fails partway through. You might see a spinner that never finishes.

### The preview looks blank or broken

Sometimes the file uploads but shows as a grey box or a broken-image icon. The website stored your file but can't display HEIC in a browser, so there's nothing to show.

In every one of these cases, the fix is the same: give the website a **JPG**.

## How to stop it happening again

If you regularly hit this wall, you can tell your iPhone to capture photos as JPG from the start:

1. Open **Settings → Camera → Formats**.
2. Tap **Most Compatible**.

From now on, new photos are saved as JPG, which uploads everywhere without conversion. The trade-offs: your photos take up more storage, and you lose some HEIC-only features like Live Photos being stored in a single file. This setting only affects *future* photos — anything already in your library stays HEIC, so you'll still convert those when you need to.

A nice middle ground is to leave the camera on **High Efficiency** (to save storage), and convert copies to JPG only when a website asks for one. You keep the space savings and still have a share-ready file whenever you need it.

## What about screenshots and downloaded images?

Worth knowing: **screenshots** on an iPhone are saved as PNG, not HEIC, so they usually upload fine. The same goes for images you've saved from the web or received in some messaging apps — they may already be JPG. HEIC is specific to photos your device's **camera** captured, so if an upload fails, that camera roll photo is the first thing to check.

Not sure what format a file is? On iPhone, open the photo, tap the **info (ⓘ)** button, and it'll show the file type. On Windows or Mac, the file extension after the dot (`.heic`, `.jpg`, `.png`) tells you.
