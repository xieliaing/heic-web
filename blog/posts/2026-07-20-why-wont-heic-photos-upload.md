You're filling out a form — a job application, an insurance claim, a marketplace listing, a school portal — and you try to attach a photo straight from your iPhone. Instead of uploading, you get an error: *"file type not supported"* or *"please upload a JPG or PNG."* The photo looks perfectly fine on your phone, so what gives?

The culprit is almost always the **`.heic`** file format. It's one of the most frequent frustrations iPhone users run into, and the good news is it's easy to fix. Here's why it happens and how to get your photo uploaded in under a minute.

## Why websites reject HEIC files

Since 2017 (iOS 11), iPhones and iPads have saved photos in **HEIC** — short for High Efficiency Image Container — by default. It's a modern, space-saving format that packs the same picture into roughly half the file size of a JPG. That's great for your phone's storage, but it creates a compatibility gap when you try to share photos elsewhere.

Many websites and upload forms simply weren't built to accept HEIC, for a few reasons:

- **The upload field only whitelists certain types.** A lot of forms are coded to accept `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, and `.png` and nothing else. Even if the site *could* display a HEIC, the form blocks it before it's uploaded.
- **The server can't process or display it.** Older content systems and image pipelines don't know how to read HEIC, so they refuse it rather than risk a broken image.
- **The site needs to show your photo to everyone.** JPG opens on virtually any device or browser made in the last 30 years. HEIC support is still catching up, especially on Windows and Android, so many sites stick with the universal formats.

In short, the photo isn't broken or corrupted — it's just saved in a newer format the website doesn't recognize. If you want the background on the format itself, we wrote a plain-English explainer on [what a HEIC file actually is](/blog/what-is-a-heic-file).

## When to pick PNG or WebP instead

JPG is the right choice about 95% of the time for uploads, but two other formats are worth knowing:

- **[PNG](/heic-to-png)** is lossless and supports transparency. Choose it if the site asks specifically for PNG, or if you're uploading a screenshot, logo, or image with sharp text and lines where you want pixel-perfect edges.
- **[WebP](/heic-to-webp)** produces the smallest high-quality files. It's ideal if *you* run the website and want fast-loading images — though double-check the destination accepts it, since some older upload forms still don't.

For everyday uploads to a form that says "JPG or PNG," stick with **JPG**. It's the safest bet.

## Prefer to stop HEIC at the source?

If you upload photos often and want to skip the conversion step entirely, you can tell your iPhone to capture JPGs from now on:

1. Open **Settings → Camera → Formats**.
2. Tap **Most Compatible** instead of *High Efficiency*.

From then on, new photos are saved as JPG and will upload straight from your library. The trade-offs: JPGs take up more storage, and you lose a few HEIC-only perks like 16-bit color and depth data. Note that this only affects **new** photos — anything already in your library stays HEIC, so you'll still want to [convert those existing files](/heic-to-jpg) when you need to upload them.

A nice middle ground: leave your camera on **High Efficiency** to save space, and convert copies to JPG only on the occasions you actually need to upload or share them.
